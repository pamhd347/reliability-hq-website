## 1. Colour Palette

### Primary Colour
**Deep Teal** — `#0D6E6E`

A sophisticated blue-green that conveys trust, expertise, and technical competence. Teal bridges the gap between the approachability of green and the professionalism of blue. It suggests precision without being cold, and stands out from the sea of generic corporate blues.

*Psychology:* Stability, clarity, expertise, forward-thinking

---

### Secondary Colour
**Slate Navy** — `#2C3E50`

A rich, dark navy that provides gravitas and anchors the brand. Use for headers, footers, and areas requiring visual weight. This colour signals authority and depth of knowledge.

*Psychology:* Authority, professionalism, depth, trust

---

### Accent Colour
**Industrial Amber** — `#E67E22`

A warm, energetic orange-amber that adds contrast and draws attention to calls-to-action. This colour nods to industrial environments (safety signage, equipment indicators) without being garish. Use sparingly for buttons, highlights, and key information.

*Psychology:* Energy, action, urgency (uptime!), warmth

---

### Neutral Colours

| Colour | Hex | Usage |
|--------|-----|-------|
| Off-White | `#F8F9FA` | Page backgrounds |
| Light Grey | `#E9ECEF` | Cards, dividers, subtle backgrounds |
| Mid Grey | `#6C757D` | Secondary text, captions |
| Charcoal | `#343A40` | Body text |

---

### Colour Rationale

This palette was designed for a **B2B technical audience**:

- **Not too playful:** No bright primaries or trendy gradients
- **Not too corporate:** Avoids sterile greys and generic blues
- **Industrial resonance:** The teal and amber echo plant floors, control rooms, and technical environments
- **High contrast:** Ensures accessibility and readability in documents, courses, and templates
- **Global appeal:** Colours work across cultures without unintended associations

## 2. Typography

### Heading Font
**Inter** — [Google Fonts](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Inter)

A modern, highly legible sans-serif designed for screens. Inter has a technical precision that suits engineering content while remaining warm and approachable. Its clean geometry conveys professionalism without coldness.

**Weights to use:** 600 (Semi-Bold) for main headings, 500 (Medium) for subheadings

---

### Body Font
**Source Sans Pro** — [Google Fonts](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Source+Sans+Pro)

Adobe's first open-source typeface, designed specifically for readability in user interfaces and long-form text. Excellent for course content, templates, and technical documentation. It pairs beautifully with Inter while providing a slightly softer reading experience.

**Weights to use:** 400 (Regular) for body, 600 (Semi-Bold) for emphasis

---

### Alternative Pairing
For a more technical feel: **IBM Plex Sans** (headings) + **IBM Plex Sans** (body)
For a warmer feel: **Nunito Sans** (headings) + **Open Sans** (body)

---

### Why These Work

- **Free and accessible:** No licensing concerns, easy to implement
- **Screen-optimised:** Critical for digital products and courses
- **Professional but not stuffy:** Avoid the "law firm" feel of serifs
- **Highly readable:** Important for technical content
- **Global character support:** Works across all languages

## 4. Brand Voice Guidelines

### Voice Attributes

The Reliability HQ brand voice is:

1. **Authoritative** — We know our stuff. Grounded in Moubray/SAE standards.
2. **Practical** — Theory is great; implementation is better.
3. **Direct** — No waffle. Respect people's time.
4. **Approachable** — Expert doesn't mean elitist.
5. **Encouraging** — We're here to help you succeed.

---

### Phrases to Use ✓

- "Here's how to actually do it..."
- "Let's break this down..."
- "Based on SAE JA1011 standards..."
- "The practical approach is..."
- "What works in the real world..."
- "Common mistake to avoid..."
- "Quick win:"
- "Pro tip:"

---

### Phrases to Avoid ✗

- "It's really quite simple..." (condescending)
- "Obviously..." (assumes knowledge)
- "As any engineer knows..." (gatekeeping)
- "The optimal paradigm..." (jargon)
- "Synergise your maintenance strategies..." (corporate speak)
- "Just follow these 47 steps..." (overwhelming)
- "Buy now or miss out!" (salesy pressure)

## 5. Logo Brief

### Concept Direction

The logo should convey:
- **Reliability & stability** — Things that don't break
- **Technical competence** — Precision, engineering mindset
- **Headquarters/Hub** — Central resource, go-to place
- **Uptime & performance** — The goal of RCM

---

### Style Suggestions

- **Minimal and clean** — Avoid complexity; must work at small sizes
- **Geometric** — Suggests precision and engineering
- **Modern but not trendy** — Should age well over 10+ years
- **Single-colour capable** — Must work in mono for documents/stamps

---

### Visual Elements to Consider

- Upward trending line or graph (performance improvement)
- Checkmark integrated into design (reliability, completion)
- Gear or cog element (subtle, not cliché)
- Abstract "R" or "HQ" monogram
- Circular badge/seal shape (suggests authority, certification)
- Pulse/heartbeat line (system health monitoring)

---

### Elements to Avoid

- Overly complex illustrations
- Literal tool imagery (wrenches, hard hats)
- Generic corporate swooshes
- Gradients that don't translate to mono
- Mascots or characters
- Anything requiring explanation

---

### Reference Styles

Look to these brands for inspiration (not copying):
- **Notion** — Clean, minimal, geometric
- **Linear** — Technical, modern, precise
- **Stripe** — Professional but not cold
- **Intercom** — Approachable B2B
- **Datadog** — Technical monitoring space

---

### Logo Lockups Needed

1. **Primary:** Full logo with wordmark
2. **Icon only:** For favicons, social profiles, app icons
3. **Horizontal:** For email signatures, narrow spaces
4. **Mono versions:** Black, white, and primary colour only

- Headings in Slate Navy or Deep Teal; body text in Charcoal
- When in doubt, simpler is better
